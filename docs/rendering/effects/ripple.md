# Live Ripple

Ripple is an image-space refractive wave, not a fluid simulation. Its field is evaluated in authored layer pixels at explicit document time. The protected disk is unchanged; a feathered annulus refracts the surrounding image. Setting the protected radius at or beyond the reach disables displacement without a singularity. Source alpha is retained at every output pixel, including transparent image edges.

The model is an original radial adaptation of the wavelength/amplitude/speed relationships described by Mark Finch in [GPU Gems, Effective Water Simulation from Physical Models](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models). The image lookup follows the efficient coordinate-perturbation principle described by Tiago Sousa in [GPU Gems 2, Generic Refraction Simulation](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-ii-shading-lighting-and-shadows/chapter-19-generic-refraction-simulation). [Apple's Ripple Transition](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coreimage/cirippletransition) provides a primary visual/control reference for a centered pond ripple; this effect does not reproduce its transition or shading implementation.

For distance r from the normalized center, the phase is `2π × (r − innerRadius − direction × speed × time) / wavelength + phaseDegrees × π/180`. A sinusoid displaces the source in the radial direction. Smooth inner/outer gates and a distance-power falloff limit its amplitude. Both gates are exactly zero inside the protected disk and outside the outer radius. Feather widths are bounded by half the annulus width, with a 0.01px numerical minimum that remains representable at the maximum legal radius. The center direction divides by a positive epsilon, producing zero displacement rather than an undefined normal. Optional dispersion separates red/blue lookups by up to a quarter of the radial displacement; zero dispersion retains a single refractive color coordinate. All texture lookups use the shared orientation-aware source sampler.

There is no random field or implicit clock. The same parameters, explicit time and source reproduce the same pixels. Preview and export share the common fixed-reference effect plane and GPU mip reduction; zoom does not change the wave's reach, wavelength, phase or protected region. The radial gizmo edits the same center/reach/protected-radius settings through ordinary validated document commands.

Spatial and temporal phase are reduced modulo one cycle independently before they are combined. This avoids subtracting tens of millions of cycles from a fractional spatial coordinate in float32, which would erase the wave. At the legal limits `time=86400`, `speed=1024`, `wavelength=2`, the clock advances exactly 44,236,800 cycles: its output must equal time zero without losing the active annulus. The remaining arithmetic follows the renderer's float32 precision; this is not an arbitrary-precision simulation clock.

## Visual acceptance

The inspected primary visual is Apple's [Ripple Transition sample](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/GraphicsImaging/Reference/CoreImageFilterReference/Art/CIRippleTransition_2x.png): a centered circular disturbance bends the original detail around a bounded ring. Its image transition and bright spherical shading are outside this effect's contract. Ripple instead retains one photograph, adds repeated moving concentric refraction and leaves the protected subject unchanged.

`test/browser/ripple-effect.spec.ts` uses the repository's real waterfall photograph, an off-center protected disk, a bounded active annulus and optional color separation. Its native PNGs must show curved displacement of existing photographic detail rather than generated rings painted on a flat source. The fixed protected disk and exterior are compared to the neutral source exactly; the annulus must change visibly. Both backend exports retain every source-alpha byte, reproduce deterministic explicit time, and match the actual editor GPU canvas at 50%, 100% and 200% density. Zero amplitude, collapsed/reversed radii, maximum radii with zero feather, frozen speed, opposite travel direction and extreme displacement are checked separately. Evidence PNGs and reports belong under ignored `artifacts/ripple/`; visual review of those outputs is required before acceptance.

The October 28, 2026 native run passed both backend cases on Chromium 156, Linux, Intel Xe-LPG WebGPU, viewport 1600×1000, DPR 1. The photograph's original size was 1280×960; native preparation added graded alpha before rendering. All 98 resulting alpha levels were preserved exactly. The protected disk and exterior had maximum byte error zero; the active annulus changed by a mean 21.710 RGB byte levels, and changing explicit time changed it by 23.414. Every decoded editor/export RGBA byte matched at 50%, 100% and 200%, and every 1× reference pixel matched its 2×2 export block. The four disabled/collapsed edge cases were exact no-ops. Reversing travel with equivalent phase differed in one byte by one level, within floating-point rounding. Actual WebGPU/WebGL/Canvas readback hooks all recorded zero production reads, and neither backend emitted a page error.

Visual inspection of the neutral and processed native PNGs confirmed concentric bending of the waterfall and vegetation around the untouched central detail. The distortion fades into the unchanged photograph and does not paint independent ring colors. The WebGPU and fallback outputs were visually identical; inspection-only decoding of their full 256×256 processed PNGs found a maximum inter-backend difference of one byte level (mean 0.002659 across RGBA channels). These are correctness witnesses, not frame-rate or latency measurements.
