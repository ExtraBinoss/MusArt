# Circle

Circle is a static, adjustable photographic circle raster. A rotated square grid samples the source photograph once per cell. Its luminance drives circular dot radius; polarity chooses shadows or highlights, gamma reshapes the tone response, and spacing, dot size and softness control the graphic screen. Ink and paper have independent colors and opacities. This is a stylized circular screen, not a calibrated printing or area-exact press simulation.

The primary visual reference is Apple's [CIDotScreen sample](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/GraphicsImaging/Reference/CoreImageFilterReference/Art/CIDotScreen_2x.png), inspected alongside its [center, angle, width and sharpness controls](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coreimage/cidotscreen). Adobe's [Color Halftone documentation](https://helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/desktop/effects-filters/artistic-stylize-filters/apply-distortion-and-stylize-filters.html) independently describes radius and screen angle. The implementation is an original circle-distance field, not copied filter code.

Coordinates use authored document pixels with a Y-down center-relative rotation. For cell luminance L, shadow tone is 1−L, highlight tone is L, and radius is `spacing × dotSize × sqrt(tone^gamma) / sqrt(2)`. Thus white has no shadow dot, dark cells grow toward a fully covered square, and changing zoom cannot alter grid registration. Inverse rotation maps each cell's center back to the same source sampler. The 0.001px minimum transition width avoids equal smoothstep endpoints; zero dot size explicitly disables ink. The entire result uses the existing shared 1× reference and GPU mip reduction.

Ink/paper opacities multiply the original per-pixel alpha and can only reduce its coverage. They cannot paint outside the original silhouette. The amount control blends premultiplied colors and alpha, then returns straight RGB to the common effect pipeline; amount zero selects the exact original sample, including fractional alpha. Opacity zero has a protected denominator and produces transparent black without NaN. No resource, clock, seed or random field is added. Seed and time are not offered as controls and do not change this static screen.

Native acceptance uses a real photograph with graded alpha, distinct light/dark source controls and known circles. It must prove larger shadow dots for darker cells, polarity inversion, cell spacing/rotation/softness changes, exact neutral amount, requested ink/paper colors and opacity, no alpha expansion, deterministic static output, and byte-identical editor/export at matched densities. Screenshots/PNGs are inspected against the primary reference; reports and images belong under ignored `artifacts/circle/`.

## Native evidence

The October 28, 2026 run passed both backend cases on Chromium 156, Linux, Intel Xe-LPG WebGPU, viewport 1600×1000, DPR 1. Its real 1280×960 photograph was prepared natively with graded alpha. All 98 resulting source-alpha levels were retained exactly with opaque ink/paper. Reducing ink opacity to 60% and paper opacity to zero capped alpha at 153 without expanding any source pixel's coverage; both opacities zero produced an entirely transparent result, and amount zero restored the source exactly.

At spacing 16, one fixed 16×16 cell in the white/gray/black controls contained respectively 0/208/256 ink pixels. Highlight polarity reversed the white/black cases. Gamma 2 reduced the gray cell to 96 ink pixels; softness 4 produced 120 intermediate edge pixels. A quarter-opacity #306090 paper decoded to [48,96,143,64], with the expected single-level straight-RGB rounding from RGBA8 premultiplication. Rotation and spacing independently changed the registered screen.

Every decoded RGBA byte of the actual editor GPU canvas matched its same-density PNG at 50%, 100% and 200%; every 1× reference pixel matched its corresponding 2×2 export block. Repeated static output was independent of seed/time. All three production readback probes recorded zero, with no page errors. These are correctness observations rather than render-latency measurements.

Visual inspection of the photographic output, tone control and transparent-ink result confirmed literal round dots, merged dark regions, preserved bright detail and the independently chosen blue/cream ink/paper palette. The pattern agrees with the primary reference's variable-dot screen structure while preserving this implementation's documented radius response.
